Every page of this site carries a line in the top left: roasted daily, in Austin,
Texas. Nobody needs that information. The software is delivered over a wire, the
clients are wherever they are, and no part of the work requires me to be in a
particular city.

So it's worth saying why it's there.

## A place is a constraint, and constraints are honest

Coffee packaging tells you where the beans came from because it changes what's
in the bag. Altitude, rainfall, who picked it. The origin isn't marketing, it's
disclosure — it tells you what you're actually buying.

Software has almost no equivalent. You usually can't tell where a thing was made,
who made it, how many of them there were, or whether anyone is still awake at the
other end. The industry has spent twenty years sanding those details off, and
the result is a lot of products that feel like they came from nowhere and answer
to no one.

Putting a city on the page is a small refusal of that. It's a claim you can check.
There is a person, in a place, in a timezone, and that person is reachable.

## What it commits me to

An address is a promise about availability. Austin is US Central, which means my
working day overlaps most of North America and the first half of Europe's
afternoon, and doesn't meaningfully overlap Asia. That's a real limit and I'd
rather state it than let you find out during a bad week.

It also sets the size of the thing. One person in one city cannot take on
unlimited work, and a studio that names a street it could be found on is
implicitly admitting it doesn't scale. That's the point. The roster stays short
because the hands stay singular.

## The unglamorous version

There's a version of this paragraph that gets misty about the city — the music,
the tacos, the heat that makes you reconsider your choices from May onward. All
of that is true and none of it is why the line is on the page.

The line is on the page because software that comes from somewhere, made by
someone who says who they are, is a different product from software that doesn't.
Not necessarily better. But accountable in a way the other kind isn't, and the
whole studio is an argument that accountability is worth the loss of scale.

The globe icon next to it spins when you point at it. That part is just for fun.
